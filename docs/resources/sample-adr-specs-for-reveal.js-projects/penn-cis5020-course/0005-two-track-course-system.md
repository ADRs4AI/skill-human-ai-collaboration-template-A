# ADR 0005: Two-Track Course System Design

**Status**: Accepted (Experimental)
**Date**: 2026-01-22
**Iteration**: 1
**Context**: Creating environment for genuine engagement while acknowledging student constraints

---

## Decision

CIS 5020 operates as a **two-track system** where students choose between independent study (Track 1) or collaborative learning (Track 2), with **grades guaranteed in both** to remove extrinsic motivation and enable intrinsic learning.

---

## Core Problem

### The De-Individualization Crisis

**Banking model effects observed** (from CIT 5920):
- Students submitting ChatGPT output with "insert your name here" still in text
- "What's the rubric?" asked for creative assignments
- 95% vs 100% creating massive anxiety
- Students who never attended, only emailed for exceptions
- Complete erosion of intrinsic motivation

**Root cause**: When grades are the only motivation, everything becomes performative. The question "What does the teacher want?" replaces "What do I want to learn?"

**Iatrogenic impact**: The very act of trying to measure learning (via grades) destroys the learning process itself.

---

## The Solution: Remove Extrinsic Motivation

### Track Structure

| Aspect | Track 1: Independent | Track 2: Collaborative |
|--------|---------------------|------------------------|
| **Grade** | A (guaranteed) | A (guaranteed) |
| **Attendance** | Optional | Mandatory* |
| **Tools** | Claude Pro + Super Whisper | Claude Pro + Super Whisper |
| **Class time** | Work independently | Discussion, pair work, dialectic |
| **Commitment** | None required | Full engagement expected |
| **Best for** | Juggling many commitments | Want deep learning experience |

*Pre-submitted exceptions allowed

---

## Track 1: Independent Study

### Philosophy

**Not a judgment**. Some students:
- Are overloaded with work, family, other courses
- Need the course for degree requirements but it's not their focus
- Learn better independently
- Have scheduling conflicts

**The offer**: Take the A, take the tools (Claude Pro, Super Whisper), work at your own pace.

**The ask**: Please don't occupy space in Track 2. If you're not going to engage fully, that's completely valid—but don't take a seat from someone who wants to be there.

---

### Track 1: What You Get

1. **Guaranteed A**
   - No exams
   - No required submissions
   - No attendance tracking

2. **Claude Pro license** (3 months, ~$60 value)
   - Access to Sonnet 4.5
   - For learning, projects, anything

3. **Super Whisper license**
   - Voice-to-text tool
   - Runs locally on Mac

4. **Access to all materials**
   - Lecture slides
   - Readings
   - Problem sets (optional)
   - Super syllabus

**No strings attached**. This is genuinely what you get.

---

## Track 2: Collaborative Learning

### Philosophy

**Problem-posing pedagogy requires everyone in the room to opt in.**

When everyone chooses to be there:
- Pair work isn't a drag (partner actually cares)
- Discussion is rich (people prepared, engaged)
- We can take intellectual risks together
- Questions are genuine, not performative

**This only works if everyone wants to be there.**

---

### Track 2: What You Get

1. **Everything from Track 1**
   - Guaranteed A
   - Claude Pro
   - Super Whisper
   - All materials

2. **Class time focused on thinking**
   - Not lecture-as-information-transmission
   - Discussion, debate, dialectic
   - Working through problems together
   - Pair programming, group problem-solving

3. **Warm Call system**
   - You sign up to be called on
   - No cold calling
   - We value your voice, but consent-based

4. **Syllabus co-creation**
   - You help shape what we cover
   - Your interests matter
   - Genuine curriculum co-design

5. **Community of practice**
   - Other students who chose to be here
   - Shared commitment to learning
   - Intellectual partnership

---

### Track 2: What's Required

1. **Mandatory attendance**
   - With pre-submitted exceptions
   - Can't just skip randomly
   - Class time is the course

2. **Come prepared**
   - Do readings
   - Think about problems
   - Bring questions

3. **Engage genuinely**
   - Not performatively
   - Can disagree with instructor
   - Can say "I don't know"
   - Can take intellectual risks

4. **Respect the opt-in culture**
   - Everyone here chose this
   - Honor that commitment
   - Contribute to shared learning

---

## Rationale: Why This Works

### QST: Won't everyone just take Track 1 for the free A?

**ANS**: Some will, yes. And that's fine. But:

1. **Self-selection** reveals true motivation
   - Students who want deep learning will choose Track 2
   - Students who don't will honestly choose Track 1
   - No more pretending

2. **Removes lying incentive**
   - In banking model: "I'm sick" (really: didn't prepare)
   - In two-track: Track 1 exists, no need to lie

3. **Creates authentic Track 2**
   - Only students who *want* to be there
   - Transforms classroom culture
   - Enables risk-taking

### QST: What if Track 2 is empty?

**ANS**: Then we've learned something valuable about:
- What students actually want
- Whether problem-posing pedagogy resonates
- Institutional constraints on student time

If Track 2 is empty, that's data, not failure. It means students are too overloaded for genuine engagement, or the value proposition isn't clear, or the culture doesn't support it.

### QST: What about students who want the A but also want to learn?

**ANS**: That's Track 2. The A is guaranteed there too. The difference is **commitment** and **engagement**, not grade outcome.

## Implementation Details

### Track Selection Process

**Week 1**: Announce system, explain both tracks

**Week 2**: Google Form for track selection
- Name
- Track choice
- (Optional) Why you chose this track
- (Optional) What you hope to get from the course

**Week 3**: Finalize rosters
- Track 1 students: receive tools, materials, done
- Track 2 students: begin collaborative work

**Can you switch tracks?**
- Track 2 → Track 1: Yes, anytime (with notice)
- Track 1 → Track 2: Harder (disrupts cohort), requires conversation

---

### Track 2: Attendance Policy

**Mandatory with exceptions**:
- Pre-submit absences via form
- Valid reasons: illness, family emergency, work conflict, religious observance, mental health
- No judgment on reasons
- Just need to know who's coming

**If repeated absences**:
- Conversation: "Are you in the right track?"
- Offer to move to Track 1 (no penalty)
- Not punishment, just alignment

**Why mandatory?**
- Class time IS the course for Track 2
- Discussion requires continuity
- Respect others who showed up

---

### Warm Call System

**Not cold calling**. Students opt in.

**Mechanism**:
- Google Form: "I'm willing to be called on about [topic] today"
- Students fill out before class or at beginning
- Instructor only calls on students who signed up

**Why this works**:
- Respects consent
- Reduces anxiety
- Still gets participation
- Students can prepare what they want to talk about

**Alternative**: Table groups discuss first, then tables volunteer

## Pedagogical Grounding

### Freire's Critique of Banking Model

**Banking model**:
- Teacher deposits knowledge
- Students withdraw on exams
- Students are passive receptacles
- De-individualization

**Problem-posing pedagogy**:
- Teacher and students co-investigate
- Learning through dialogue
- Students are active subjects
- Individuality respected

**Two-track system enables problem-posing** by:
- Removing grade coercion (everyone gets A)
- Creating opt-in space (Track 2)
- Respecting constraints (Track 1 exists)

---

### Self-Determination Theory

**Three needs for intrinsic motivation**:
1. **Autonomy**: Choice in what you do
2. **Competence**: Feeling capable
3. **Relatedness**: Connection to others

**Banking model violates all three**:
- No autonomy (prescribed curriculum)
- Competence judged by grades (external validation)
- Relatedness discouraged (competitive, isolated)

**Two-track system supports all three**:
- **Autonomy**: Choose track, co-create syllabus
- **Competence**: A guaranteed, focus shifts to mastery
- **Relatedness**: Track 2 is community of practice

## Risks & Mitigations

### Risk 1: Track 2 is too small

**If < 5 students**: May not be viable as discussion-based course

**Mitigation**:
- Set minimum threshold (e.g., 8 students)
- If below threshold, offer hybrid model
- Communicate this possibility upfront

---

### Risk 2: Track 2 students don't actually engage

**Scenario**: Students choose Track 2 but then don't prepare, don't participate

**Mitigation**:
- Warm call system (opt-in removes "forgot to prepare" excuse)
- Mid-semester check-in: "Is this working for you?"
- Offer to move to Track 1 without penalty
- Trust that most self-selection will be accurate

---

### Risk 3: Track 1 students feel less-than

**Scenario**: Track 1 seen as "giving up" or "fake students"

**Mitigation**:
- Frame Track 1 neutrally in presentation (no judgment language)
- Emphasize legitimate reasons (overloaded, different learning style)
- Make tools valuable (Claude Pro license is real)
- Don't create different Canvas groups or visible separation

---

### Risk 4: Administration pushes back

**Scenario**: "You can't just give everyone an A!"

**Mitigation**:
- Frame as experiment in addressing engagement crisis
- Document pedagogical grounding (Freire, SDT)
- Point to CIT 5920 outcome (worked well)
- Emphasize learning outcomes over grades
- Be willing to compromise (e.g., Track 1 has some async requirement)

**If required**: Add minimal Track 1 requirement (e.g., one reflection essay) to justify grade

---

### Risk 5: Students game the system

**Scenario**: Choose Track 2 for "connections" but don't engage, or bounce between tracks

**Mitigation**:
- Track switching policy (Track 2 → 1 easy, reverse hard)
- Warm call system reveals non-preparation
- Trust initial self-selection
- Accept some gaming as cost of doing business (still better than banking model)

## Assessment Philosophy

### QST: If everyone gets an A, how do you assess learning?

**ANS**: We don't, in the traditional sense. Instead:

**Track 1**:
- Learning is self-directed
- Tools are the support
- No formal assessment

**Track 2**:
- Portfolios (self-curated learning artifacts)
- Peer feedback (not grading)
- Self-reflection
- Formative assessment (Slido, discussion) for learning, not grades

**The point**: Assessment *for* learning, not assessment *of* learning for sorting/ranking.

---

### What the Instructor Actually Wants

**If students ask**: "What does the teacher want?"

**Answer**:
1. Create valuable environment for all students
2. Help you think critically
3. Support you developing your own opinion

**Not**:
- Whether you agree with me
- Whether you use "correct" terminology
- Whether you fit a rubric

**Care about**:
- Whether you're **thinking**
- Whether you're **curious**
- Whether you're developing **your** perspective

## Comparison to Other Experimental Models

### Ungrading Movement

**Similarities**:
- De-emphasize grades
- Focus on learning
- Student agency

**Differences**:
- Ungrading often has narrative grades or contracts
- Two-track is more radical: literally everyone gets A
- Two-track creates explicit opt-in space (Track 2)

---

### Spec Grading

**Similarities**:
- Clear expectations
- Multiple pathways

**Differences**:
- Spec grading still requires meeting specifications
- Two-track removes all grade pressure
- Track 1 has no specifications at all

---

### Pass/Fail

**Similarities**:
- Reduces grade pressure
- Binary outcome

**Differences**:
- Pass/fail still requires passing
- Two-track: everyone passes automatically
- Two-track separates engagement (track) from outcome (A)

## Data Collection

### What to Track

**For research/improvement**:
- Track selection numbers
- Track switching (who, when, why)
- Track 2 attendance patterns
- Warm call participation rates
- End-of-semester surveys (both tracks)
- Student learning artifacts (with permission)

**Not tracking**:
- Traditional grades (everyone gets A)
- Comparative performance (not the point)

**Research question**: Does two-track system enable deeper engagement compared to traditional grading?

## Future Iterations

### If Successful

- Refine track selection process
- Better onboarding for Track 2 norms
- Improve warm call mechanism
- Document and share model

### If Track 2 Too Small

- Adjust marketing/framing
- Add incentives (e.g., letters of rec only for Track 2?)
- Combine with hybrid model
- Accept that institutional context may not support it yet

### If Track 2 Too Large

- Good problem to have!
- May need to split into sections
- Or create sub-groups within Track 2

## Communication Strategy

### To Students (Week 1)

**Frame it as**:
- Response to observed problems in education
- Respect for your time and agency
- Experiment we're trying together
- Your choice, no judgment either way

**Don't frame as**:
- Track 1 is for "lazy" students (NO)
- Track 2 is for "serious" students (NO)
- Implying one is better than the other

**Frame as**: Different paths for different needs and goals.

---

### To Administration

**Emphasize**:
- Pedagogical grounding (Freire, SDT)
- Response to engagement crisis
- Learning outcomes focus
- Experimental nature
- Willingness to collect data and assess

**If challenged**:
- CIT 5920 precedent (worked well)
- "Everyone gets A" is end state, not starting assumption
- Can add minimal Track 1 requirement if needed

---

### To Other Faculty

**Be honest about**:
- This is experimental
- May not work
- Grounded in research but adapted for context
- Curious to see what happens
- Open to feedback and iteration

**Don't**:
- Claim this solves everything
- Judge traditional approaches
- Proselytize
