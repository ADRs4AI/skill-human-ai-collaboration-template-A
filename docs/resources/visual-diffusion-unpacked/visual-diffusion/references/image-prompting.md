# Advanced prompting taxonomy for image generation diffusion models

**This comprehensive technical taxonomy documents prompt engineering patterns, syntax, vocabulary, and techniques for major diffusion models including Nano Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image), GPT Image 1, DALL-E 3, Midjourney, Stable Diffusion, and Flux.** The taxonomy focuses exclusively on what can be controlled through prompt text itself, providing actionable frameworks for generating high-quality images across any artistic style or application. Key findings reveal that while natural language models (Nano Banana, GPT Image 1, Flux) excel with conversational prompts, syntax-based models (Stable Diffusion, Midjourney) require specific weight modifiers and parameters to achieve comparable control. Both "Nano Banana" and "GPT Image 1" exist as production models with distinct architectures: Nano Banana uses multimodal diffusion transformers with 80B parameters achieving 2.3-second generation speeds, while GPT Image 1 builds on GPT-4o's foundation supporting up to 10 input images for composition.

## Model identification and architecture

Two priority models were successfully identified and documented. **Nano Banana is the codename for Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash Image** (model ID: `gemini-2.5-flash-image`), which originated as an internal placeholder name during secret testing on LMArena in August 2025. This multimodal diffusion transformer uses separate weight sets for image and language representations, achieving 40% improvement in text understanding versus traditional diffusion models. The architecture processes images at native 1024x1024 resolution with 80 billion total parameters (13 billion active), reducing generation time by approximately 60% compared to conventional methods. A more powerful variant, Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image), was released November 2025 with 4K resolution support and advanced text rendering capabilities.

**GPT Image 1 is OpenAI's official API name** for GPT-4o's image generation capabilities, released April 2025. Built on GPT-4o's multimodal foundation, it supports high-resolution output up to 4096×4096 pixels with native image generation, editing, and inpainting capabilities. The model handles up to 10 input images for editing and composition, with exceptional instruction-following abilities that excel at complex, detailed prompts. Transparent background support enables PNG generation with alpha channels, while multi-image composition allows sophisticated visual blending.

## Universal prompt structure patterns

All diffusion models benefit from structured prompts following established patterns, though execution varies by architecture. The **standard template** across models follows: **[Medium/Frame] + [Subject] + [Style] + [Details/Modifiers] + [Quality Boosters]**. The medium defines artistic format ("A photograph of", "A digital illustration of", "An oil painting of"), establishing baseline aesthetic expectations. The subject forms the core content and should appear early in prompts, as position significantly affects influence strength—beginning words carry highest weight, middle words moderate influence, and ending words lower impact.

Academic research identifies six distinct modifier types: subject terms denoting main content, style modifiers indicating artistic approach, image prompts as visual references, quality boosters enhancing aesthetic output, repeating terms for reinforcement, and magic terms introducing unexpected creative elements. This taxonomy enables systematic prompt construction where each element serves specific purposes. For instance, "A cinematic photograph of a warrior in ornate armor, dramatic lighting, trending on artstation" structures medium (cinematic photograph), subject (warrior in armor), details (dramatic lighting), and quality boosters (trending on artstation) in optimal order.

Alternative frameworks exist but converge on similar principles. **Stability AI's SD3.5 approach** sequences style → subject/action → composition/framing → lighting/color → technical parameters, emphasizing front-loading of critical elements. The academic Scale.com framework simplifies to Frame + Subject + Style + optional Seed, proving effective for straightforward generations. Natural language models like DALL-E 3 and Nano Banana handle more flexible structures, automatically parsing conversational descriptions into structured representations internally.

## Syntax systems and weight modifiers

Syntax capabilities diverge sharply between model families. **Stable Diffusion's AUTOMATIC1111 syntax** provides granular control through nested parentheses and brackets. Single parentheses multiply weight by 1.1x: `(keyword)` becomes 1.1×, `((keyword))` becomes 1.21× (1.1²), and `(((keyword)))` reaches 1.331× (1.1³). Explicit weights offer precision: `(keyword:1.5)` applies exactly 150% emphasis, while `(keyword:0.5)` reduces to 50%. Brackets decrease emphasis with `[keyword]` at 0.9× and `[[keyword]]` at 0.81×. Practical ranges span 0.5 to 1.5—values exceeding 2.0 typically degrade quality.

Advanced Stable Diffusion techniques include **prompt scheduling** with syntax `[keyword1:keyword2:factor]`, switching at specified generation percentages. At step (total_steps × factor), the model transitions from keyword1 to keyword2. Early steps control global composition while later steps refine details, enabling effects like `[baroque:cyberpunk:0.3]` that starts baroque and transitions to cyberpunk at 30% completion. The **BREAK keyword** (uppercase) forces new 75-token chunks, preventing color and attribute bleeding across concepts: "impressionist landscape BREAK cyberpunk city" maintains distinct style regions.

**Midjourney's parameter system** operates entirely differently, placing all parameters after prompt text with `--parameter value` format. Multi-prompts use double colons for weight distribution: `sunset over ocean:: mountains in background::0.5` assigns default weight 1 to sunset and 0.5 to mountains. Critical parameters include `--ar 16:9` for aspect ratios, `--stylize 0-1000` controlling artistic interpretation (0 for literal, 1000 for maximum artistry), `--chaos 0-100` varying generation consistency, and `--quality 0.25-2` balancing speed versus detail. Style references (`--sref [URL]`) and character references (`--cref [URL]`) enable consistency across generations.

**Natural language models** (Nano Banana, GPT Image 1, DALL-E 3, Flux) reject special syntax entirely. Weight modifiers like parentheses or brackets produce no effect—these architectures interpret prompts conversationally. Emphasis emerges through descriptive language: instead of `(red rose:1.5)`, use "vibrant crimson rose" or "with strong emphasis on the red rose." Flux documentation explicitly states that traditional Stable Diffusion syntax fails; prompts should read as natural explanations to an artist. This fundamental architectural difference means prompt portability across model families requires complete restructuring.

## Technical vocabulary for visual control

**Style control** vocabulary spans artistic movements, periods, and descriptors. Classical references include Renaissance (realistic representation, classical forms, perspective), Baroque (dramatic lighting, rich colors, intense emotions), Impressionism (loose brushwork, capturing light, visible strokes), and Surrealism (dreams, unconscious mind, fantastical imagery). Contemporary terms like hyperrealism, photorealism, cyberpunk, steampunk, and vaporwave evoke specific aesthetic packages. Combining movements produces novel blends: "Art Nouveau portrait with Cyberpunk elements" merges organic decorative lines with neon futurism.

Artist names serve as powerful style modifiers when models recognize them. **High-recognition classical masters** include Leonardo da Vinci, Michelangelo, Rembrandt, and Caravaggio. **Impressionist and Post-Impressionist** references like Monet, Van Gogh, Renoir establish painterly aesthetics. **Contemporary digital artists** particularly effective in prompts include Greg Rutkowski (detailed textured fantasy art), Alphonse Mucha (Art Nouveau decorative patterns), Artgerm/Stanley Lau (polished character art), and Moebius/Jean Giraud (clean-lined sci-fi illustration). Format as "by [Artist Name]", "in the style of [Artist Name]", or "[Artist Name] style". Combining multiple artists blends characteristics: "by Moebius, Killian Eng, Leonardo da Vinci" fuses European comic aesthetics with Renaissance technique.

**Composition vocabulary** structures spatial relationships and visual flow. Rule of thirds places subjects at intersection points of a 3×3 grid, while golden ratio (1:1.618 divine proportion) and Fibonacci spirals create mathematical harmony. Balance types include symmetrical composition (mirror balance), asymmetrical balance (visual equilibrium without mirroring), and radial symmetry (emanating from center). Advanced techniques employ leading lines (directing viewer's eye), negative space (emptiness creating focus), framing (environmental elements surrounding subject), and depth layering (distinct foreground, midground, background planes). Headers should describe spatial relationships: "centered", "off-center weighted left", "tight framing", "foreground interest with background blur".

**Lighting descriptors** dramatically transform mood and atmosphere. Studio lighting terms include three-point lighting (key, fill, backlight), rim lighting (edge glow separating subject from background), softbox lighting (diffused even illumination), and ring light (circular catchlight in eyes). Natural lighting varies by time: golden hour (warm soft light shortly after sunrise or before sunset), blue hour (cool twilight tones), midday sun (harsh overhead), and magic hour (optimal photography conditions). Dramatic effects emerge from volumetric lighting (visible light beams), god rays (crepuscular shafts), chiaroscuro (strong light-dark contrast), and tenebrism (extreme dramatic darkness). Color temperature matters: "warm golden lighting" versus "cool blue moonlight" versus "neutral even illumination".

**Camera terminology** provides technical precision for photographic realism. Shots by distance include extreme wide shot/establishing shot (location context), wide shot/full shot (complete subject and environment), medium shot (waist up), close-up (head and shoulders), and extreme close-up (facial details). Angles transform perception: eye-level (neutral perspective), high-angle (looking down, diminishes subject), low-angle (looking up, empowers subject), bird's-eye view (directly overhead), worm's-eye view (ground level upward), and Dutch angle (tilted, creates tension). Lens specifications affect rendering: wide-angle lens (15-35mm) expands space with distortion, normal lens (50mm) mimics human vision, telephoto lens (85-300mm) compresses distance and creates smooth bokeh, macro lens (extreme closeup with blurred background), and fish-eye lens (extreme spherical distortion).

Depth of field controlled through aperture mentions: **shallow depth of field** (f/1.4, f/1.8, f/2.8) blurs backgrounds beautifully for portraits, while **deep depth of field** (f/11, f/16, f/22) maintains sharpness throughout for landscapes. "Bokeh" refers specifically to aesthetic quality of out-of-focus areas. Adding camera brands influences style: "shot on Leica" (cinematic quality), "Hasselblad medium format" (commercial photography), "Fujifilm XT4" (film-like colors), "Canon 5D" (professional standard). Film grain, motion blur, tilt-shift effects, and long exposure references add technical authenticity.

**Artistic medium terms** define material and technique simulation. Traditional painting includes oil painting (rich textures, impasto thick paint, visible brushstrokes), watercolor painting (transparent washes, bleeding colors, soft edges), acrylic painting (vibrant flat colors), gouache (opaque matte finish), and tempera (egg-based Renaissance technique). Drawing media span pencil sketch, charcoal drawing (dramatic blacks, smudging), ink drawing (precise lines), pen and ink, colored pencil, and pastel drawing (soft chalky colors). Digital and 3D terms include digital painting, concept art, matte painting, 3D render, Octane render, Unreal Engine, Cinema 4D, Blender render, and physically-based rendering (PBR). Low-poly and high-poly specify geometric complexity, while isometric and voxel art reference specific digital aesthetics.

**Color vocabulary** enables precise palette control. Color theory terms include hue (pure color), saturation/chroma (color intensity), brightness/luminance/value (lightness), and tone (hue plus grey). Color harmony systems specify relationships: analogous colors (adjacent on wheel), complementary colors (opposite on wheel), triadic colors (equally spaced), split-complementary, and monochromatic (single hue variations). Descriptors like vivid, vibrant, saturated increase intensity, while muted, desaturated, pastel reduce it. Warm tones (reds, oranges, yellows) versus cool tones (blues, greens, purples) versus neutral tones versus earth tones establish mood. Color grading terms like "cinematic color grading", "teal and orange", "film look", "LUT applied", "Technicolor", and "Kodachrome" reference specific palettes. Iridescent, metallic, pearlescent, opalescent effects add surface complexity.

**Quality and detail modifiers** boost output fidelity. Resolution terms include 4K, 8K, 16K, ultra HD, high definition, and high resolution. Detail levels escalate through highly detailed, ultra-detailed, extremely detailed, hyperdetailed, intricate details, fine details, meticulous, elaborate, and sharp focus. Realism modifiers include photorealistic, hyperrealistic, ultra-realistic, lifelike, true-to-life, realistic lighting, realistic textures, and physically accurate. Professional quality terms reference award-winning photograph, Pulitzer Prize photography, National Geographic style, gallery quality, museum quality, editorial quality, and commercial photography. For 3D/CGI, rendering quality markers include Octane render, Unreal Engine render, ray traced, path traced, global illumination, subsurface scattering, and high-poly model. Platform references like "trending on Artstation", "featured on Behance", and "Artstation HQ" serve as quality boosters.

## Model-specific prompting techniques

**Nano Banana (Gemini 2.5 Flash Image)** excels with natural conversational prompts following the pattern "Create/Generate an image of [subject] [action] [scene]". The model's multimodal diffusion transformer architecture achieves 0.89 prompt adherence (GenEval benchmark) and maintains character features across edits. Strengths include multi-turn conversational editing for iterative refinement, processing up to 5 images simultaneously (14 images for Pro version), and superior text rendering—Nano Banana Pro achieves 94% character accuracy for in-image text. Effective prompting emphasizes specificity: "Create an image of a young woman in a red dress running through a park" outperforms "Create an image of a woman in a red dress." Multi-step editing works exceptionally well with explicit instruction lists: "Make ALL of the following edits: Put a strawberry in the left eye socket, Put a blackberry in the right eye socket, Put a mint garnish on top, Change the plate to a chocolate-chip cookie." For text rendering, specify exact content and placement: "with the text 'Hello World' written in bold letters on the storefront sign." Scene preservation prompts like "maintaining the original background" or "preserving facial features" ensure controlled edits. Technical specifications produce authentic results: "shot on iPhone", "DSLR photograph", "chiaroscuro lighting", "golden hour with warm ambient light".

**GPT Image 1 (OpenAI)** handles exceptionally detailed prompts and complex instructions through its GPT-4o foundation. The model supports granular API parameters enabling precise control: `quality` settings (low/medium/high affect detail and cost), `size` options (1024×1024, 1024×1536, 1536×1024, auto), `background` (opaque/transparent for PNG alpha channels), and `output_format` (JPEG/PNG). Detailed character specifications work brilliantly—structured descriptions like "Render a realistic image of this character: Name: Glorptak, Body Shape: Amorphous and gelatinous teardrop silhouette, Material Texture: Semi-translucent bio-luminescent goo, Surface: Ripples when communicating" produce consistent results. Image editing via masking allows surgical modifications: upload multiple images with alpha channel masks marking edit regions, then prompt changes like "Put the crown on the T-rex's head." Transparent background generation either through explicit prompt ("A pixel-art green bucket hat with pink quill on transparent background") or API parameter (`background="transparent"`) produces clean assets. Multi-image composition combines up to 10 images: "Combine the images of the cat and the hat to show the cat wearing the hat while perched in a tree, in pixel-art style." The model particularly excels with technical photography specifications ("85mm lens at f/1.8, natural window lighting"), studio-lit rendering ("studio-lit render of sci-fi helmet with neon etching, photorealistic materials, 4K quality"), and architectural descriptions ("Victorian greenhouse with iron filigree, overgrown with jasmine vines, golden hour with cinematic warm lighting").

**DALL-E 3 (OpenAI)** benefits from automatic prompt enhancement through ChatGPT integration—simple prompts expand into detailed descriptions automatically. This synthetic captioning training enables strong understanding of context, object relationships, and background elements. Prompt enhancement transforms "futuristic AI robot" into "A futuristic AI robot, showcasing advanced technology and design. The robot is humanoid in shape but with clearly mechanical features, glowing blue LED eyes, chrome metallic finish, standing in a high-tech laboratory with holographic displays." Natural language works best: "A serene Japanese garden in autumn, peaceful atmosphere, warm golden light filtering through maple trees with falling red leaves, stone path with moss, traditional wooden bridge over koi pond." For text generation (experimental capability), specify exact wording and font: "A vintage neon sign that reads 'Open 24 Hours' in script font, glowing against a dark brick wall, with some letters flickering." Artistic style references integrate naturally: "In the style of Vincent van Gogh's Starry Night, with swirling brushstrokes and vibrant blues and yellows creating dynamic movement across the canvas." The model requires 2-3 generation attempts for reliable text results. Comma separation helps parsing: "Retro science fiction book cover, bold chrome lettering reading 'Space Explorers', diagonal dynamic layout, starfield background with planet, 1960s pulp aesthetic." DALL-E 3 lacks weight modifier support—emphasis emerges through descriptive language rather than syntax.

**Midjourney (v7/v6/v5)** operates exclusively through Discord bot or web interface with all control via parameters appended after prompt text. Essential parameters include `--ar [width]:[height]` for aspect ratios (16:9, 1:1, 2:3, 3:2), `--quality 0.25|0.5|1|2` balancing speed versus detail, `--stylize 0-1000` controlling artistic interpretation (0 for literal prompt adherence, 1000 for maximum Midjourney aesthetic), `--chaos 0-100` varying generation consistency, `--style raw` removing Midjourney's default aesthetic for literal interpretation, and `--version 7|6.1|6|5` selecting model version. Advanced parameters include `--seed 0-4294967295` for reproducible results, `--iw 0.5-2` controlling image prompt weight, `--sref [URL]` for style reference from images with `--sw 0-1000` controlling style weight, `--cref [URL]` for character reference maintaining facial consistency, `--no text, watermark, signature` as shorthand negative prompt, `--tile` creating seamless patterns, and `--repeat 5` generating multiple variations. Multi-prompts use double colons for weight distribution: "sunset over ocean:: mountains in background::0.5" assigns weights to different concepts. Effective Midjourney prompts stay concise with high-signal phrases: "A modern commercial building with glass facade in crowded urban area at sunset --ar 16:9 --q 2 --stylize 750 --chaos 30" combines natural description with technical parameters. Artist names, platform references ("trending on artstation"), render engines ("Octane render", "Unreal Engine"), and photography terms all work exceptionally well. Parameters must always follow text/images with no punctuation inside parameter syntax.

**Stable Diffusion (SD 1.5, SDXL, SD3)** uses CLIP text encoders processing 75-token chunks with emphasis syntax providing fine control. Parentheses increase weight: `(word)` = 1.1×, `((word))` = 1.21×, `(word:1.5)` = explicit 1.5×. Brackets decrease weight: `[word]` = 0.9×, `[[word]]` = 0.81×, `[word:0.5]` = explicit 0.5×. Plus/minus syntax in some interfaces: `word+` = 1.1×, `word++` = 1.21×, `word-` = 0.9×. Don't over-emphasize—weights above 2.0 typically degrade quality. Prompt scheduling transitions between keywords: `[baroque:cyberpunk:0.3]` starts baroque, switches to cyberpunk at 30% generation. BREAK keyword (uppercase) forces new 75-token chunks preventing attribute bleeding: "portrait of woman with red hair BREAK blue dress BREAK golden sunset background" separates color assignments. LoRA inclusion uses syntax `<lora:filename:0.8>` applying custom training with specified weight. Textual inversion embeddings reference as `<embedding:name>`. Effective SD prompts structure quality terms prominently: "masterpiece, best quality, highly detailed, 8k, photorealistic, portrait of woman with (((perfect eyes))), ((detailed facial features)), dramatic lighting, by Rembrandt, sharp focus, professional photography." Negative prompts prove essential, particularly for V2 models: "ugly, deformed, blurry, low quality, bad anatomy, poorly drawn, ((bad hands)), extra fingers, missing limbs, watermark, text, signature, worst quality." SDXL handles natural language better than SD 1.5 with reduced dependence on quality boosters and artist names. SD3.5 excels at text rendering when using double quotes: "storefront sign reads \"BAKERY\"" produces legible text.

**Flux (Flux.1 Pro/Dev/Schnell)** rejects all special syntax—parentheses, brackets, plus/minus, and weight modifiers produce no effect. This Black Forest Labs model uses hybrid architecture combining multimodal and parallel diffusion transformer blocks with both T5 XXL and dual CLIP models (G/14 and L/14), totaling 12 billion parameters. Natural language excellence allows conversational prompts: "A Victorian-era library with floor-to-ceiling bookshelves, leather armchairs, warm fireplace glow illuminating dust particles in the air, rich mahogany wood, persian rugs, peaceful scholarly atmosphere." For emphasis, use descriptive language: instead of `(red rose:1.5)`, write "vibrant crimson rose" or "with strong focus on the red rose." Comprehensive prompt template follows: [Subject] + [Technical Foundation] + [Environmental Details] + [Lighting Interaction] + [Style/Mood] + [Composition]. Example: "A mountain climber ascending a snow-covered peak, golden sunrise light catches crystalline ice formations creating ethereal alpenglow effect, shot with 24mm wide-angle lens, dramatic composition with vast sky dominating upper two-thirds." Technical specifications work excellently: "Captured with 35mm lens at f/1.8, shallow depth of field focusing on foreground subject, background softly blurred with bokeh effect, natural window light from left side." Material and texture descriptions produce realistic surfaces: "Weathered bronze statue with green patina, showing intricate details and texture, sunlight creating dramatic highlights and shadows on carved surface." Flux particularly excels at transparent object rendering: "A hanging glass terrarium featuring miniature rainforest scene with colorful orchids and tiny waterfalls, with 'Rainforest Retreat' visible through the glass in elegant serif font." Prompt length limits reach 512 tokens maximum. Three versions balance quality and speed: Flux.1 Pro (best quality, API-only, commercial use), Flux.1 Dev (open-weight, non-commercial, similar Pro quality), Flux.1 Schnell (fastest generation, Apache 2.0 license, local use).

## Advanced techniques for control and consistency

**Negative prompts** specify unwanted elements, working by conditioning the unconditioned latent to move away from undesired features. Syntax remains standard text separated into negative prompt field in most UIs. Weight modifiers apply identically to positive prompts: `(blurry:1.4)` strongly emphasizes avoiding blur, `(extra fingers:0.8)` moderately reduces extra fingers. Effective negative prompts balance quality issues, anatomical problems, and style exclusions. **Universal quality base** includes "ugly, deformed, disfigured, bad anatomy, poorly drawn, blurry, low quality, low resolution, watermark, signature, username, text, error, cropped, worst quality, jpeg artifacts." For portraits add "bad face, asymmetrical face, bad eyes, crossed eyes, lazy eye, deformed eyes, bad hands, extra fingers, fewer fingers, missing fingers, fused fingers, bad proportions, extra limbs, missing limbs." For photorealistic images add "cartoon, 3D, anime, illustration, drawing, painting, unrealistic, fantasy, abstract" to exclude artistic styles. Start small with 2-3 terms, expanding based on persistent issues. Model-specific behavior varies—V2 Stable Diffusion heavily depends on negative prompts while V1.5 works adequately without them. Negative embeddings like "easynegative", "bad-artist", "FastNegative" encapsulate multiple negative concepts into single trigger words, streamlining workflow.

**Style mixing** combines multiple aesthetic influences through various techniques. Concatenation simply lists artists or styles: "by Moebius, Killian Eng, Leonardo da Vinci" blends their characteristics with order influencing dominance. Weighted style mixing uses Midjourney multi-prompt syntax: `art by leonardo da vinci::2 banksy::1 jackson pollock::1` gives 2× weight to da Vinci. Stable Diffusion employs parentheses weights: `(Moebius style:1.4), (Killian Eng:0.8), (cyberpunk aesthetic:1.2)` precisely controls influence. LoRA weights enable trained style mixing: `<lora:moebius_style:0.7> <lora:killian_eng:0.5>` applies multiple custom-trained styles. Prompt scheduling transitions styles during generation: `[baroque:cyberpunk:0.3]` starts baroque, transitions to cyberpunk at 30% sampling. BREAK keyword separates style regions: "impressionist landscape BREAK cyberpunk city" prevents bleeding. Platform and medium references inject stylistic packages: "trending on artstation" (polished digital art), "pixiv" (anime/manga aesthetics), "deviantart" (fan art diversity), "unreal engine" (3D rendered look), "octane render" (photorealistic 3D). Multi-artist combinations produce novel fusions: "portrait by Yoshitaka Amano, Range Morata, Katsuya Tarata" merges Japanese illustrators, "comic art style of Moebius and Frank Miller" fuses European and American comics, "photography by Annie Leibovitz, Helmut Newton" blends fashion photography masters.

**Seed control** enables reproducibility and systematic variation. Seeds are numbers (0 to 4,294,967,295) initializing randomness—identical seed plus identical settings and prompt produces identical image (mostly, with some model variance). Default setting typically random (-1 in AUTOMATIC1111). Practical applications include reproducing exact images for iteration, varying only prompts while keeping seed constant to test modifications, varying single parameters to isolate effects, creating character expression variations while maintaining appearance, and sharing exact images with seed plus settings. Variation seed features (AUTOMATIC1111 "Extra" checkbox) enable controlled variations: variation seed plus variation strength (0-1) modifies base seed. Low strength (0.2-0.4) produces subtle changes maintaining composition, high strength (0.7-0.9) generates significant changes while preserving essence. Limitations include model-specific behavior (different models produce different results with same seed), scheduler and sampler sensitivity (different settings alter output), and resolution dependence (image size changes significantly affect results). Seeds don't appear directly in prompts—they're parameters. For character consistency, generate 100-200+ images with same prompt, cluster and identify consistent appearances, select 5-20 best matches, then use as training data or reference set.

**Aspect ratio control** occurs via parameters rather than prompt text, though indirect prompt influence exists. SDXL optimizes for 1024×1024 with common ratios 16:9, 1:1, 21:9, 2:3, 3:2, 4:5, 5:4, 9:16, 9:21 set via `aspect_ratio` parameter. SD 1.5 optimizes for 512×512 or 512×768 with width/height divisible by 8—portrait typically 512×768 (2:3), landscape 768×512 (3:2). Midjourney uses `--ar 16:9` or `--aspect 16:9` with common presets. Square (1:1) works best as training baseline; rectangular images may exhibit composition issues. Extreme ratios (32:9) risk quality degradation. Indirect prompt influence uses mentions like "portrait orientation", "panoramic landscape", "square composition" as hints but proves unreliable—always use parameters for control.

**Character consistency techniques** span prompt-based methods to advanced training. Using famous faces as base leverages known identities: "Natalie Portman as a 25 year old female scientist, blue hair, white lab coat" maintains consistency through celebrity anchor. Gender/ethnicity swap trick uses male celebrities for female characters: `[Chris Pratt | Henry Cavill] as a 25 year old gorgeous Thai female mechanic` with negative prompt "male, man" produces consistent novel character. Detailed character description creates "identity anchors" specifying age, gender, ethnicity, facial features (jaw structure, eye color, face shape), hairstyle and color (specific: "buzzed haircut", "long silver hair", "wispy bangs"), body type ("jacked", "thicc", "tall", "petite"), clothing (specific colors and items), and expression/personality traits. Structured format helps: "Identity: Ava, a tall warrior elf with long silver hair, sharp green eyes; Appearance: wearing dark leather armor with silver accents; Setting: in moonlit forest; Style: cinematic lighting, detailed."

Seed and parameter control maintains consistency by using same seed number, consistent CFG scale (typically 7-12), same model/checkpoint, and consistent sampling steps (20-50). Advanced techniques provide superior consistency: **LoRA training** (recommended) balances efficiency and quality, requiring 10-30 images with 5-10 minute training producing small files (10-300MB) applicable to any base model. Process: generate character sheet with multiple poses, cut out individual poses, train LoRA on 10-30 images, use LoRA in future prompts with trigger word. **Textual Inversion/Embedding** creates new vocabulary tokens the model understands, requiring 5-30 images with small file size working atop any model, though less consistent than LoRA but faster. **DreamBooth training** provides most powerful results but demands significant resources, retraining entire model with 2-4GB file size per character, best quality and flexibility but requiring substantial GPU resources. **ControlNet methods** include OpenPose (skeleton/pose reference), Depth (depth map for composition), and IP-Adapter (reference image for style/character) maintaining spatial relationships while varying style.

Character sheets generate turnarounds: "character sheet, multiple poses, white background, front view, side view, back view, 3/4 view, white background, reference sheet" creates comprehensive references. Use ControlNet OpenPose for consistent poses across generations. Generate variations in different scenarios using img2img with low denoising (0.3-0.5) for consistency. Prompt chaining for video/sequences changes only 1-2 elements per frame, maintains core identity description, and uses latent reuse preserving features: Frame 1 "Ava standing, sword at rest", Frame 2 "Ava standing, raising sword slightly", Frame 3 "Ava standing, sword raised high" progresses smoothly.

## Controlling specific visual elements

**Face control** addresses expressions, features, and ethnicities through specific vocabulary. Expression keywords include smiling, laughing, angry, serious, contemplative, sad, surprised, neutral, terrified, joyful, melancholic. Feature descriptors specify "detailed eyes", "sharp jawline", "round face", "chiseled features", "soft features", "high cheekbones", "full lips", "button nose". Ethnicity specifics use "Asian features", "African features", "European features", "Latin features", "Middle Eastern features" with age indicators "youthful", "middle-aged", "elderly", "wrinkled", "smooth skin", "age lines". Common face problems require targeted fixes: garbled or distorted faces benefit from face restoration (CodeFormer, GFPGAN) with adjustable weight (0.5-1.0), inpainting masked face areas at higher resolution, Adetailer extension for automated detection and fixing, Hi-Res Fix generating at 512px then upscaling 2× for more pixels, and improved VAE (Stability AI's patch) fixing rendering issues. Positive prompt additions include "detailed facial features", "perfect eyes, detailed eyes", "symmetrical face", "high detail face", "sharp focus on face". Negative prompt additions prevent issues: "deformed face, ugly face", "asymmetrical face", "bad eyes, crossed eyes", "poorly drawn face".

**Hand accuracy** remains challenging due to complex bone structure (27 bones), numerous possible positions, limited training data with correct hands, and high variability in poses. Positive prompts specify "beautiful hands", "detailed fingers", "natural hand position", "5 fingers", "perfect hands", "anatomically correct hands". Specific poses include "hands at sides", "hands behind back", "hands crossed", "hands holding [object]", "fist", "pointing", "waving", "open palm". Comprehensive negative prompts prevent issues: "bad hands, unnatural hands, disfigured hands, deformed hands, extra fingers, fewer fingers, fused fingers, too many fingers, mutated hands, poorly drawn hands, bad anatomy, missing fingers, extra digits." Fixing strategies include inpainting hand areas with prompt "detailed hand with 5 fingers, natural pose" generating multiple versions, ControlNet OpenPose using reference image with correct hand skeleton guiding structure, ControlNet Depth using depth map showing hand structure for better finger separation, img2img with reference photo at low denoising (0.3-0.5) transferring hand structure, and hiding hands strategy using "hands behind back", "hands in pockets", gloves or jewelry, strategic cropping, or objects covering hands. Specialized tools include OpenArt Fix Hand Feature for automated correction, Photoshop Generative Fill for post-processing, and hand embeddings like "Bad Hands Five" in negative prompts. Prevention strategies use portrait orientation (less likely showing full hands), simple poses (standing, arms at sides), avoiding complex gestures initially, generating multiple images selecting best hands, and using anatomy-trained models like Protogen.

**Text rendering** historically challenged Stable Diffusion but improves in modern models. SD 3.5, GPT-4o, and Nano Banana Pro handle text best, working well with 1-2 lines specifying exact text in quotes. Structure: "[Description of scene], text that reads \"[EXACT TEXT]\" in [font style] font, [additional styling]." Example: "Bakery storefront with vintage wooden sign that reads 'Butter & Crust' in gold serif font, carved wood, warm lighting." ControlNet plus text image method creates text in image editor (black text, white background), uses ControlNet Depth with "invert" preprocessor, prompts desired style while model fills text shape with texture. Process: create text in Paint/Photoshop, load in ControlNet, set preprocessor "invert", set model "control_sd15_depth", prompt "paint dripping, vivid colours, in the style of Alberto Seveso", Control Weight 0.8-1.2, generate. Inpainting method creates plain text image programmatically, creates masks for text and background, inpaints separately with different prompts. Advanced typography control uses ETC-tokens for formatting: `<b*>text</b*>` for bold, `<i*>text</i*>` for italic, `<u*>text</u*>` for underline, providing word-level control over typographic features. Style Control Adapter (SCA) injects style via image input independent from text content for better artistic text rendering.

Font specifications guide typography: "serif font" (traditional formal), "sans-serif font" (modern clean), "handwritten calligraphy" (artistic flowing), "bold letters" (strong prominent), "vintage lettering" (retro aged), "script font" (cursive flowing), "monospace font" (technical), "display font" (decorative headlines). Placement keywords include "at the top", "centered", "in the corner", "across the bottom", "integrated into scene". Integration phrases specify "naturally integrated", "blending with scene", "carved into [surface]", "painted on [surface]", "neon sign", "graffiti text", "etched into metal". Negative prompts for text prevent issues: "blurry text, illegible text, garbled letters, wrong text, misspelled, distorted text, unreadable." SD3.5 uses double quotes for text rendering: "storefront sign reads \"BAKERY\"" produces legible results.

**Background control** manages detail levels, blur, and depth through specific keywords. Detail level control uses "detailed background" (high detail throughout), "simple background" (minimal distraction), "blurred background" (focus on subject), "bokeh" (camera blur effect), "white background" (studio/clean), "gradient background" (color transition), "black background" (dramatic isolation), "transparent background" (asset creation). Depth of field control specifies rendering focus: shallow depth of field uses "shallow depth of field, bokeh, f/1.4, f/2.8, cinematic depth, subject in focus, background blur, soft focus background" blurring backgrounds beautifully for portraits, while deep depth of field employs "deep depth of field, f/16, f/22, sharp focus throughout, everything in focus, landscape photography" maintaining sharpness throughout for landscapes. Specific depth techniques include "foreground in focus, background blurred" isolating subjects, "middle ground blur" isolating between planes, "rack focus effect" for selective focus, and "tilt-shift" creating miniature effects.

Background replacement workflow uses Rembg extension plus inpainting (install Rembg extension in AUTOMATIC1111, use to create subject mask, inpaint background with new prompt, optional ControlNet Depth for better edges), ControlNet Depth (preserves spatial relationships, clear foreground/background separation, preview depth map before generating, adjust control weight 0.7-1.0), or Depth-to-Image (enhanced img2img using depth information, three conditionings: prompt/image/depth, better subject/background synthesis with process: upload original image, model generates depth map via MIDaS, prompt new scene, generates with depth preserved). Background style prompts span nature backgrounds ("forest scene with misty fog", "beach at sunset, golden hour", "mountain landscape, dramatic clouds"), urban backgrounds ("cyberpunk city, neon lights", "busy city street, blurred motion", "industrial warehouse interior"), fantasy/sci-fi ("ethereal forest, bioluminescent plants", "space station interior, holographic displays", "magical library, floating books"), and abstract/conceptual ("geometric patterns, vibrant colors", "color field, gradient blue to purple", "bokeh lights, dreamy atmosphere").

Lighting control for backgrounds uses natural lighting terms—"golden hour lighting" (warm soft), "blue hour lighting" (cool twilight), "harsh midday sun" (strong shadows), "overcast, soft shadows" (even lighting), "god rays through trees" (dramatic beams)—and artificial lighting—"studio lighting, three-point", "rim lighting" (edge glow), "volumetric lighting" (visible light beams), "neon lighting" (colorful glow), "candlelight" (warm flickering). Camera settings affect background rendering: "wide angle lens" (more background visible), "telephoto lens" (compressed, less background), "85mm lens" (portrait, natural blur), "50mm lens" (balanced view), "macro lens" (extreme closeup, very blurred background).

**Scene and environment consistency** maintains coherent locations across generations. Location anchors create consistent environments through detailed descriptions: "Location: Gothic cathedral interior, vaulted stone ceilings, stained glass windows casting colored light, marble floors, ornate columns, dim atmospheric lighting with rays of light." ControlNet maintains consistency: ControlNet Depth (maintains spatial layout, consistent room geometry, reliable foreground/background separation, use across multiple generations), ControlNet Canny edge detection (preserves structural lines, maintains architectural features, consistent object placement), ControlNet Semantic Segmentation (color-coded regions ensuring consistent scene layout, good for landscape consistency). Regional Prompter extension divides images into regions assigning different prompts maintaining coherence. Lighting consistency maintains same conditions: time of day ("midday harsh sunlight" versus "twilight soft glow"), weather ("overcast diffused light" versus "bright sunny day"), quality ("warm candlelight" versus "cool fluorescent"), direction ("side lighting" versus "front lit"). Color palette consistency specifies schemes: "warm color palette, oranges and yellows", "cool tones, blues and teals", "monochromatic, shades of grey". Style consistency uses consistent modifiers: "cinematic lighting, film grain, 35mm", "painterly style, visible brushstrokes", "photorealistic, 8K, highly detailed".

## Taxonomy of artistic styles and movements

Artistic styles organize chronologically and categorically providing systematic navigation of aesthetic possibilities. **Classical and Renaissance periods** (pre-1600s) include Classicism (harmony, proportion, balance, simplicity inspired by Ancient Greece and Rome), Italian Renaissance (realistic representation, classical forms, perspective), and Mannerism (elongated figures, exaggerated poses, unnatural lighting, vibrant colors). **Baroque to Romanticism** (1600-1850) spans Baroque (dramatic lighting, rich colors, intense emotions, ornate detail), Rococo (ornate decorative, pastel colors, light elegant), Neoclassicism (return to classical ideals, balanced composition, rational simplicity), and Romanticism (emotional expression, dramatic scenes, nature themes).

**Modern movements** (1850-1945) revolutionized aesthetics through Realism (accurate everyday life depiction), Impressionism (loose brushwork, capturing light and atmosphere, visible strokes), Post-Impressionism (emotional expression, bold colors, distinct styles like Van Gogh's swirls), Art Nouveau (sinuous lines, organic forms, decorative 1890-1910), Fauvism (wild vibrant colors, bold brushstrokes, expressive color use), Expressionism (subjective art, distorted forms, emotional intensity), Cubism (geometric shapes, multiple perspectives, abstract forms), Futurism (dynamism, speed, energy, technology themes), Dadaism (anti-art absurdist, challenges conventions), Surrealism (dreams, unconscious mind, fantastical imagery), De Stijl (simple geometry, black/white/primary colors, abstract), Constructivism (abstract geometric, propaganda-fueled Russian Revolution), and Art Deco (grandiose geometric patterns, luxury 1920s).

**Contemporary movements** (1945-present) include Abstract Expressionism (spontaneous gestures, emotional expression, non-representational), Pop Art (mass culture references, bold colors, repetition, commercial imagery), Minimalism (reduction of elements, clean lines, functional design), Conceptual Art (emphasis on concept over form), and Contemporary Art (current era art spanning Banksy, Jeff Koons, Takashi Murakami). Digital and technological styles emerged: Digital Art, 3D rendering, Glitch Art, Vaporwave (nostalgic 80s/90s internet aesthetic), Cyberpunk (dystopian high-tech, neon cities), Steampunk (Victorian-era industrial technology), Pixel Art (8-bit/16-bit retro gaming), Low Poly (minimalist geometric 3D), and Photobashing (combining photos and digital painting).

Style categories organize by medium and technique: traditional painting (oil painting, watercolor, acrylic, gouache, tempera), drawing media (pencil, charcoal, ink, pastel, colored pencil), printmaking (etching, lithograph, screen print, woodcut, linocut), photography styles (portrait, landscape, street, fashion, documentary, macro, architectural), digital techniques (digital painting, concept art, matte painting, photo manipulation), 3D rendering (low-poly, high-poly, PBR, ray tracing), and illustration (vector art, comic book, manga/anime, editorial, children's book). Visual characteristics provide another organizational axis: photorealistic (hyperdetailed realistic rendering), abstract (non-representational shapes and colors), stylized (simplified forms, artistic interpretation), painterly (visible brushstrokes, artistic rendering), flat design (minimalist 2D graphics), cinematic (film-like lighting and composition), and fantasy/sci-fi (imaginative futuristic magical).

Cultural and regional styles include Japanese aesthetics (Ukiyo-e woodblock prints, Manga, Anime, Kawaii cute culture, Wabi-Sabi imperfect beauty), Chinese traditions (ink wash painting, calligraphy, literati painting), European traditions (Gothic, Byzantine, Dutch Masters, French Impressionism), African art (tribal masks, geometric patterns, bold colors), Art Nouveau movements across cultures, and Indigenous art styles. Temporal styles reference decades: 1920s Art Deco luxury, 1950s atomic age optimism, 1960s psychedelic counterculture, 1970s disco aesthetics, 1980s neon excess, 1990s grunge and early digital, 2000s Y2K futurism, 2010s flat design minimalism, 2020s maximalist eclecticism.

## Best practices and systematic approaches

Successful prompting follows systematic principles across all models. **Prompt structure formula** sequences elements for maximum impact: [Subject] + [Action/Pose] + [Attributes] + [Environment] + [Lighting] + [Camera] + [Style] + [Artist Reference] + [Quality Modifiers]. Example: "A young woman warrior, holding sword raised high, long red hair flowing, silver armor with blue accents, standing in ancient ruins, dramatic sunset lighting, low angle shot, cinematic, in the style of Frank Frazetta, highly detailed, 8K, sharp focus." Positive and negative prompts work in tandem—positive describes desired content while negative prevents issues.

**Word order priority** significantly affects output as beginning words carry more weight than ending words. Optimal sequence places most important subject/character first, key actions or poses second, critical attributes third, environment fourth, style and artistic references fifth, and technical quality terms last. Front-loading strategy ensures primary concepts dominate: "Warrior in ornate armor, dramatic pose" outperforms "Dramatic pose, warrior in ornate armor" by emphasizing character over action. Iterative development process starts simple, generates 4+ images assessing baseline, adds 1-2 modifiers at a time, generates batch evaluating impact, iterates based on results, and builds personal library of effective patterns. Documentation tracking successful prompts and seeds enables reusable templates for common needs.

**Keyword selection best practices** favor concrete specific nouns over abstract concepts, include technical photography terms when needed, reference specific artists/styles, add lighting descriptors, specify composition/framing, use comma separation for distinct concepts, and test keyword effectiveness in isolation. Avoid mixing conflicting styles, using abstract concepts as main subject, overstuffing with redundant modifiers, assuming all popular keywords work without testing, neglecting negative prompts, and forgetting word order importance. Quality descriptor vocabulary spans universal quality ("masterpiece, best quality, highly detailed, sharp focus, professional, 8K, ultra quality, award-winning, intricate, stunning, beautiful, elegant, refined, polished"), negative quality ("ugly, deformed, disfigured, poor quality, blurry, bad anatomy, worst quality, low resolution, jpeg artifacts"), and specific modifiers for realism ("photorealistic, hyperrealistic, ultra-realistic, lifelike, physically accurate, detailed skin pores, realistic materials").

**Universal negative prompt library** provides foundation: "ugly, deformed, disfigured, bad anatomy, poorly drawn, blurry, low quality, low resolution, watermark, signature, username, text, error, cropped, worst quality, jpeg artifacts." For portraits add "bad face, asymmetrical face, bad eyes, crossed eyes, lazy eye, deformed eyes, bad hands, extra fingers, fewer fingers, missing fingers, fused fingers, bad proportions, extra limbs, missing limbs." For realistic images add "cartoon, 3D, anime, illustration, drawing, painting, unrealistic, fantasy, abstract." Model-specific optimization adjusts complexity: SD 1.5 (base resolution 512×512, works well with multiples of 64, artist names highly effective, needs strong negative prompts), SDXL (base resolution 1024×1024, better text understanding, less need for artist names, improved anatomy), SD3.5 (best text rendering, improved prompt adherence, better multi-subject handling, enhanced typography).

Training and fine-tuning methods balance quality, speed, and resources. Textual Inversion produces files under 1MB with 1-2 hour training on 8GB+ GPU requiring 5-30 images, achieving medium quality for quick concepts. LoRA (recommended) creates 10-300MB files with 5-30 minute training on 8GB+ GPU requiring 10-30 images, achieving high quality as best balance. DreamBooth produces 2-4GB files with 1-3 hour training on 12GB+ GPU requiring 10-30 images, achieving highest quality but most resource-intensive. Hypernetwork generates 10-100MB files with 2-4 hour training on 8GB+ GPU requiring 20-50 images, achieving medium quality primarily for styles. Start with LoRA training for optimal balance of quality, speed, and file size.

Community resources accelerate learning through prompt databases (Lexica.art for searching SD prompts and images, PromptHero for curated library, Civitai for models and prompts community, Stable Diffusion Art website for comprehensive guides), artist reference sites (Stable Diffusion Cheat Sheet on GitHub for visual artist reference, artist comparison tools on NightCafe, Google Arts & Culture for art movement research), and essential tools (AUTOMATIC1111 as most popular GUI, ComfyUI for node-based workflow, Adetailer for automatic face/hand fixing, ControlNet for advanced conditioning, Regional Prompter for multi-region control, Rembg for background removal).

This comprehensive taxonomy provides systematic framework for mastering prompt engineering across all major diffusion models, enabling precise control over any artistic style, application, or visual requirement through text prompts alone.
